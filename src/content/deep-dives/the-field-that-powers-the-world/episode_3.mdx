## Poynting Vector

Maxwell's equations described how electric and magnetic field create each other. But they didn't explicitly show where the energy travels through space. Henry Poynting (in 1884) filled that gap with a simple, beautiful equation:

$$
\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\,\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}
$$

Showing energy doesn't flow along the electric field or along the magnetic field. It flows perpendicular to both, which is exactly what the cross product (×) captures mathematically.

This is the revelation: energy flows through the electromagnetic field in the space surrounding the wire. But it doesn't just flow past the conductor but couples into it such that field configuration creates an electric field *inside* the wire, and that's what actually moves the electrons.

The copper wire isn't a pipe carrying energy. It's a guide that shapes the electromagnetic field in space and carrying energy outside, directing electrons inside.

But a wave, once launched, doesn't need anyone to keep pushing it. Light
from a star still travels long after the star itself is gone.

So why does this field need anything to sustain it at all?

Because this loop, E creating B, B creating E, isn't launched once and
left alone. Something has to keep feeding it. Something has to keep the
imbalance from collapsing.

Something refusing, relentlessly, to let go.

## the Complete Picture 

So, when you flip the switch and the circuit closes, all of this happens at once.

The electromagnetic field restructures itself throughout the circuit. The Poynting vector springs into existence, mapping how energy moves through space. An internal electric field emerges inside the wire. Electrons begin drifting, in one direction, together.

Energy flows outside the wire, carried by the field through empty space. Current flows *inside* the wire, guided by that same field. At the bulb, the two converge — energy absorbed, current delivered, light produced.

Two jobs. One unified structure.

Without the wire: the field propagates, but there are no charges to do work.

Without the field: the wire sits inert, charges with no instructions.

Together: the battery maintains the imbalance, the field carries the energy through space and directs the current, the wire hosts the charges and shapes the field's geometry and electrons drift, quietly, to deliver power to the bulb.

The room floods with light.

That's electricity. An electromagnetic structure, visible only in its effects, guiding slow charges through the geometry we built with copper and glass.
