### Yes.

This is where the electric field becomes much stranger. <br />A static electric field around an isolated charge needs a source charge. But an electric field does not always need a charge sitting where the field exists.

A changing magnetic field can create an electric field, even in completely empty space. Faraday discovered this experimentally:
$$
\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}
$$
And Maxwell took the idea further: changing electric and magnetic fields can sustain one another and propagate through space as an electromagnetic wave. <br />That's how light can travel through a vacuum.
<br />So, the more complete picture is,
> Charge can create electric fields, but fields don't always need nearby charges.
