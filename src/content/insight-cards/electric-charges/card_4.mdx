You might imagine charge working like water: keep adding a smaller and smaller amount until you have any value you want. Nature doesn't quite work that way.

Electric charge comes in discrete units:

$$
q = ne
$$

where $n$ is an integer and

$$
e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}
$$

An electron carries (−e). A proton carries (+e). <br />Quarks complicate the picture: they carry charges of $+\frac{2}{3}e$ or $-\frac{1}{3}e$. but they are confined inside composite particles and aren't observed as isolated charges.

**Why does nature have these particular charge units?** That's where the question stops being simple particle bookkeeping and reaches the quantum structure of the Standard Model.
